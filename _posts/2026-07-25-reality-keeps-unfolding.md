I want to return to the thought in [Reality just keeps unfolding](https://ckarchive.com/b/mvu7h5hqdzmd7uv5oo45wirxvvqq3i3hw9v2e), from *The Imperfectionist*. Even the title captures something I find intellectually healthy: reality is not a closed object we simply possess once the right theory is written down, but something we continue to meet through inquiry, experiment, interpretation, and revision.

This attitude matters in physics and philosophy. It leaves room for formal precision without turning precision into premature finality. A theory can be extraordinarily successful and still leave us with questions about what is being represented, what counts as observation, and how the agent who uses the theory fits into the story.

For me, the useful lesson is not skepticism for its own sake, but a disciplined openness. One can take theories seriously while resisting the temptation to treat current conceptual pictures as the last possible word.
