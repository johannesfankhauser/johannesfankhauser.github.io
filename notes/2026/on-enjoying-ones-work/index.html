On Enjoying One's Work

Created on July 25, 2026

2026   ·   academic-life   research   reading   ·   reading-notes

I recently returned to Terence Tao’s short note Enjoy your work. Its central reminder is simple but easy to lose sight of: serious intellectual work requires sustained effort, and sustained effort is much easier to give when one’s motivation is tied to real sources of satisfaction rather than rare external rewards.

For research, this seems especially important. There are many temptations to outsource one’s sense of direction: fashion, prestige, visible productivity, or the hope of a decisive breakthrough. But most days of research are not breakthrough days. They are days of trying to understand a small point more clearly, reformulating a question, noticing that an argument does not quite work, or helping someone else see a structure that had been obscure.

Those smaller forms of progress are not merely consolation prizes. They are often the actual substance of the work. A healthy intellectual life needs taste, patience, and contact with questions that feel worth returning to even when no immediate result is in view.

This is also one reason I find conversations, seminars, and good expository writing so valuable. They can remind us that research is not only a race for outcomes, but also a way of keeping alive certain forms of attention.

Source: Terence Tao, Enjoy your work.
